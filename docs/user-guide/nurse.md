# Nurse guide

You triage patients before they see the doctor: vital signs and allergies, then hand them over.

## Today

**Today** shows how many patients are **Waiting for triage**, **In triage** and **Ready for doctor**, and the **Longest wait**. The **Triage** panel lists patients in queue order, with emergencies first. The page refreshes itself every 30 seconds.

The list shows each patient's latest vitals, who took them and how long ago, so the doctor sees the same before calling them.

## Triaging a patient

1. Click **Triage** next to the patient (or **Continue** if you already started). You can also start from the **Queue** page.
2. **Vitals:** click **Record vitals** and enter what you measured: temperature, blood pressure, pulse, respiratory rate, SpO₂, weight and height. Leave blank what you did not measure. Click **Save vitals**.
   - NuvaLyf calculates BMI and flags readings as **Low**, **High**, **Critically low** or **Critically high** using your clinic's ranges. Tell the doctor about anything critical straight away.
3. **Allergies:** ask every patient.
   - For each allergy, click **Add allergy**: the **Substance**, the **Medicine class** if it is a medicine (for example penicillins), **Severity** and **Reaction**, then **Record allergy**.
   - If they have none, click **No known allergies** and confirm.
4. **Triage notes:** enter the **Chief complaint** and any **Nursing notes**, then **Save triage notes**. The doctor reads them first.
5. **Contact details:** use **Update contact** if the phone or address has changed.

You can read the prescription and the doctor's notes, but not change them.
   - To fix a mistake, click **Correct** on the allergy. The original stays in the history.
4. Click **Ready for doctor** and confirm **Hand over**. The patient moves to the doctor's queue as **Ready for doctor**.

Allergies you record are checked automatically every time a doctor prescribes.

## After the consultation

Once the doctor signs, vitals and notes for that visit are locked. Record any new readings at the patient's next visit.
